THE BOMBAY PROHIBITION ACT, 1949
(Bombay Act XXV of 1949)
[As originally enacted]

An Act to introduce prohibition of the manufacture, sale, and consumption of liquor and intoxicating drugs in the State of Bombay.

CHAPTER I - PRELIMINARY

Section 1. Short title, extent and commencement.
(1) This Act may be called the Bombay Prohibition Act, 1949.
(2) It extends to the whole of the State of Bombay.
(3) It shall come into force at once.

Section 2. Definitions.
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
(a) "article" means any substance or thing specified under Section 24A;
(b) "foreign liquor" means liquor imported into India from any country outside India;
(c) "illicit liquor" means any liquor or any intoxicating drug which is manufactured without a licence or in contravention of the conditions of any licence;
(d) "intoxicating drug" means any substance which the State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare to be an intoxicating drug;
(e) "liquor" includes spirits, wine, beer, toddy, and all liquids consisting of or containing alcohol;
(f) "manufacture" includes every process, whether natural or artificial, by which any liquor or intoxicating drug is produced or prepared;
(g) "permit" means a permit granted under this Act.

CHAPTER V - OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

Section 65. Penalty for illegally manufacturing or bottling liquor.
Whoever in contravention of the provisions of this Act, or of any rules, regulations, or orders made thereunder—
(a) manufactures any liquor; or
(b) constructs or works any distillery or brewery; or
(c) bottles any liquor for sale;
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years and with fine which may extend to two lakh rupees;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with fine which may extend to five lakh rupees.

Section 66. Penalty for consuming liquor or intoxicating drug.
Whoever in contravention of the provisions of this Act or any rules, regulations, or orders made thereunder, consumes or uses any liquor or any intoxicating drug—
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months or with fine which may extend to five hundred rupees, or with both;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment which may extend to one year and fine which may extend to five thousand rupees.

Section 68. Penalty for selling or buying liquor.
Whoever sells or buys, or offers or accepts for sale any liquor or any intoxicating drug in contravention of the provisions of this Act—
shall, on conviction, be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years and with fine which may extend to twenty-five thousand rupees.

Section 81. Penalty for being found drunk.
Whoever is found in a state of intoxication in any public place or in any place to which the public have or are permitted to have access—
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment which may extend to six months.

Note: The original 1949 Act had comparatively lenient penalties. The Act was a cornerstone of the Bombay Presidency's prohibition policy, reflecting the Gandhian influence on state policy regarding temperance and prohibition.
