THE BOMBAY PROHIBITION ACT, 1949
(Bombay Act XXV of 1949)
[As amended by the Maharashtra Amendment Act, 2016 and subsequent amendments]

An Act to introduce prohibition of the manufacture, sale, and consumption of liquor and intoxicating drugs in the State of Maharashtra.

CHAPTER I - PRELIMINARY

Section 1. Short title, extent and commencement.
(1) This Act may be called the Bombay Prohibition Act, 1949.
(2) It extends to the whole of the State of Maharashtra.
(3) It shall come into force at once.

Section 2. Definitions.
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires—
(a) "article" means any substance or thing specified under Section 24A;
(b) "foreign liquor" means liquor imported into India from any country outside India;
(c) "illicit liquor" means any liquor or any intoxicating drug which is manufactured without a licence or in contravention of the conditions of any licence, or any denatured spirituous preparation used for human consumption;
(d) "intoxicating drug" means any substance which the State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare to be an intoxicating drug, including any synthetic narcotic substance;
(e) "liquor" includes spirits, wine, beer, toddy, and all liquids consisting of or containing alcohol;
(f) "manufacture" includes every process, whether natural or artificial, by which any liquor or intoxicating drug is produced or prepared, and includes blending, mixing, and reconstitution of any liquor from concentrate or powder;
(g) "permit" means a permit granted under this Act;
(h) "online sale" means any sale, advertisement, or solicitation for sale of liquor through electronic means including websites, applications, and social media platforms.

CHAPTER V - OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

Section 65. Penalty for illegally manufacturing or bottling liquor.
Whoever in contravention of the provisions of this Act, or of any rules, regulations, or orders made thereunder—
(a) manufactures any liquor; or
(b) constructs or works any distillery or brewery; or
(c) bottles any liquor for sale; or
(d) possesses any equipment capable of manufacturing liquor without a valid licence;
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with fine which may extend to five lakh rupees;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to ten years and with fine which may extend to ten lakh rupees.

Section 65A. Penalty for possession of illicit liquor or intoxicating drug.
[Inserted by Maharashtra Amendment]
Whoever in contravention of the provisions of this Act or of any rules, regulations or orders made thereunder, possesses any liquor or any intoxicating drug shall, on conviction, be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees. The burden of proving lawful possession lies on the accused.

Section 66. Penalty for consuming liquor or intoxicating drug.
Whoever in contravention of the provisions of this Act or any rules, regulations, or orders made thereunder, consumes or uses any liquor or any intoxicating drug—
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months or with fine which may extend to one thousand rupees, or with both;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment which may extend to three years and fine which may extend to twenty-five thousand rupees.

Section 66(1-B). Penalty for causing death by consumption of illicit liquor.
[Inserted by Maharashtra Amendment, 2016]
Whoever manufactures, sells, or distributes any denatured spirituous preparation, or illicit liquor, the consumption of which causes death of any person, shall be punished with death or imprisonment for life and shall also be liable to fine which shall not be less than two lakh rupees.

Explanation: This section shall apply even if the death occurs at any time after the consumption of such illicit liquor and is attributable, directly or indirectly, to such consumption.

Section 68. Penalty for selling or buying liquor.
Whoever sells or buys, or offers or accepts for sale any liquor or any intoxicating drug in contravention of the provisions of this Act—
shall, on conviction, be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years and with fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees.

Section 68A. Penalty for online sale of liquor.
[Inserted by Maharashtra Amendment]
Whoever advertises, solicits, sells, or facilitates the sale of liquor through any electronic means, including websites, mobile applications, or social media platforms, without a valid permit or licence, shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years and with fine which may extend to ten lakh rupees.

Section 81. Penalty for being found drunk.
Whoever is found in a state of intoxication in any public place or in any place to which the public have or are permitted to have access—
shall, on conviction, be punished—
(i) for the first offence, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months;
(ii) for any subsequent offence, with imprisonment which may extend to one year.

Note: The Maharashtra Amendment significantly enhanced penalties across all prohibition offences. Key changes include: (1) Section 65 penalties increased from 3/5 years to 5/10 years; (2) New Section 65A for possession; (3) Section 66(1-B) introducing death penalty for hooch deaths; (4) Section 66 penalties doubled; (5) Section 68 penalties enhanced from 3 years to 5 years; (6) New Section 68A for online liquor sales; (7) Section 81 penalties increased. These amendments were driven by repeated hooch tragedies causing mass casualties in Maharashtra.
